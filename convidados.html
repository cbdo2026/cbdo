XXIII Brazilian Colloquium on Orbital Dynamics

André Izidoro Ferreira da Costa

Rice University, USA

André Izidoro Ferreira da Costa was born on November 30, 1984, in Fernandópolis, São Paulo, Brazil. He received his B.Sc. in Applied and Computational Mathematics from São Paulo State University (UNESP), in São José do Rio Preto, followed by an M.Sc. and a Ph.D. in Physics and Dynamical Astronomy at UNESP, in Guaratinguetá, under the supervision of Othon Cabo Winter. During his Ph.D., he also spent one year as a visiting student at the Institute for Astronomy of the University of Hawai‘i, working with Nader Haghighipour.

He held postdoctoral positions at the Observatoire de la Côte d’Azur in Nice, under the supervision of Alessandro Morbidelli, and at the University of Bordeaux, under the supervision of Sean N. Raymond. From 2016 to 2020, he returned to UNESP as a FAPESP Young Investigator. He joined Rice University in 2020 as a postdoctoral researcher, where he was also a Welch Foundation Postdoctoral Fellow. In 2023, he was appointed tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Department of Earth, Environmental and Planetary Sciences at Rice University, in Houston, Texas.

His research focuses on planetary formation and evolution, including terrestrial planet accretion, the delivery of water and volatiles to Earth-like planets, the origin and structure of the asteroid belt, exoplanet dynamics, and the physics of protoplanetary disks.

Among his distinctions, he received the Raynor L. Duncombe Prize for Student Research from the Division on Dynamical Astronomy of the American Astronomical Society, the FAPESP Young Investigator Award, and the Wagner Sessin Award for scientific contributions to astronomy in Brazil. In 2023, asteroid (28932) Izidoro was named in his honor by the International Astronomical Union.

Ernesto Pérez Chavela

Instituto Tecnológico Autónomo de México, Mexico

Ernesto Pérez Chavela was born on May 8, 1957, in Tuxpan, Veracruz, Mexico. He completed his undergraduate and master’s degrees at the Faculty of Sciences of the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), receiving the Gabino Barreda Medal for the highest academic performance in his master’s programme. He obtained his Ph.D. from the Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana (UAM) in 1991, under the supervision of Ernesto A. Lacomba, and was awarded the University Merit Medal for outstanding performance in the doctoral programme. He subsequently held a postdoctoral position at Northwestern University, under the supervision of Don G. Saari.

He is currently Full Professor in the Department of Mathematics at the Instituto Tecnológico Autónomo de México (ITAM). He is a member of the Mexican Academy of Sciences and, since January 2023, Emeritus Researcher of Mexico’s National System of Researchers (SNI), in which he had held the highest level since 2010.

His research focuses on Hamiltonian systems, celestial mechanics, dynamical systems, and nonlinear differential equations. He is particularly known for his work on the n-body problem in spaces of constant curvature, developed in collaboration with Florin Diacu and Manuele Santoprete, which provided a unified framework for studying the motion and existence of several classes of relative equilibria.

He has also made significant contributions to the study of central configurations in the n-body problem, relative equilibria under different potentials, and special cases of Saari’s conjecture. He has published more than one hundred papers in leading journals, supervised undergraduate, master’s, and doctoral students, and served on the editorial boards of several journals, including as Editor-in-Chief of the Bulletin of the Mexican Mathematical Society.

Giuseppe Pucacco

Università degli Studi di Roma Tor Vergata, Italy

Giuseppe Pucacco was born on July 27, 1956, in Italy. He obtained his Ph.D. in Astrophysics from the University of Rome “La Sapienza”, with a thesis entitled “Anisotropy in Cosmology and Galactic Dynamics”. Since 1989, he has been Associate Professor in the Department of Physics at the University of Rome “Tor Vergata”, where he is currently qualified for promotion to Full Professor in Mathematical Physics.

His research focuses mainly on Hamiltonian dynamical systems and their applications to celestial mechanics, galactic dynamics, astrophysics, and classical and relativistic mechanics. His main contributions include the study of resonant multi-body dynamics, orbits around the collinear points of the three-body problem, orbits in elliptical galaxies, the integrability of selected classes of Hamiltonian systems, and tests of General Relativity using laser-ranged satellites.

He is co-author, with D. Boccaletti, of the book Theory of Orbits, a general introduction to analytical mechanics with emphasis on perturbation methods. He has participated in several major research projects, including LARASE, HERMES, collaborations with INFN related to the LAG experiment and the LISA gravitational-wave mission, and the European Training Network STARDUST.

He teaches Celestial Mechanics, Dynamical Systems, and Acoustics at the University of Rome “Tor Vergata” and serves as didactic coordinator of the second-level master’s programme in Space Science and Technology. He is a member of GNFM, INFN, IAPS, and SIMCA.

Sylvio Ferraz-Mello

Universidade de São Paulo, Brazil

Sylvio Ferraz-Mello was born on October 26, 1936, in São Paulo, Brazil. He received his B.Sc. in Physics from the University of São Paulo in 1959 and his Docteur ès Sciences Mathématiques from the Académie de Paris/Sorbonne in 1966. As an undergraduate student, he began working as a technician at the São Paulo Observatory, where he was drawn to astronomy by Professor Abrahão de Moraes.

After returning from France, he joined the Instituto Tecnológico da Aeronáutica (ITA), where he established Brazil’s first graduate programme in astronomy in 1968. He remained at ITA as Full Professor until 1975, when he moved permanently to the University of São Paulo. He is currently Professor Emeritus at the Institute of Astronomy, Geophysics and Atmospheric Sciences of the University of São Paulo (IAG-USP). He directed IAG from 1981 to 1985 and served as Director of the National Observatory from 1999 to 2001.

He is a founding member of the Brazilian Astronomical Society, a Full Member of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences, and one of the founders of the Brazilian Colloquium on Orbital Dynamics. Throughout his career, he held visiting or temporary positions at several institutions, including universities in Paris, La Plata, Córdoba, Porto, Vienna, Cambridge, and Texas, as well as the Nice Observatory. He also served as President of the IAU Commission on Celestial Mechanics.

His research spans several decades and topics, including artificial satellites, the dynamics of Jupiter’s satellites, perturbation theory, variable stars, time-series analysis, asteroid dynamics, chaos in celestial mechanics, exoplanets, and planetary tides. His work on asteroid orbital resonances, including the Kirkwood gaps and the concentration of asteroids in the 3:2 resonance with Jupiter, stands among his most influential contributions.

He served as Editor-in-Chief of Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy from 2001 to 2017 and authored or edited several books and conference proceedings. His distinctions include the Grand Cross of the Brazilian National Order of Scientific Merit, the title of Doctor Honoris Causa from the Paris Observatory, and the Brouwer Award of the AAS Division on Dynamical Astronomy. The asteroid 1983 XF (5201) Ferraz-Mello was named in his honor by the International Astronomical Union.
